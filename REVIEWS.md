## Entry R1 — Validation period was truncated to 1 year despite 10 years of usable data

**Date:** 2026-08-25
**Trigger:** User noticed the validation period (2023 only) seemed too short and asked for it to be audited against the underlying HYPE modelling results.
**Status:** ✅ Fixed and propagated to figures, tables, and manuscript text.

### Finding

HYPE was actually run continuously from **2016-01-01 to 2025-12-31** (10 years) for every one of the 27 catchments and all 4 scenarios — confirmed from `bdate`/`cdate`/`edate` in `Results/model_setups/catchments/{Catchment}/hype_data*/info.txt`, identical across every catchment checked (Aurajoki, Karjaanjoki, Porvoonjoki, Sirppujoki, Vantaanjoki). The raw per-subcatchment HYPE output files (e.g. `hype_data_subcatchments/0001001.txt`) confirm daily output exists for the full 2016–2025 range.

Despite this, the post-processing pipeline only ever used a 5-year window (2019 warm-up, 2020–2022 calibration, 2023 validation) and treated 2016–2018 and 2024–2025 as unused/excluded ("Other" in the old header comment of `scripts/01_compute_gof_metrics.R`).

Cross-checking observed discharge completeness per catchment/year (`calib_disch_data_results.csv`) showed:
- **2023–2025**: near-complete coverage (usually >90%, often >99% of days) at **all but 2 of the ~30 gauge records** (Sipoonjoki and Urpalanjoki — see R4). This period was sitting unused.
- **2016**: missing entirely at 3+ gauges (Hounijoki both stations, Siuntionjoki 2200100, Vantaanjoki 2104900), which is almost certainly why 2019 — not 2016 — was originally chosen as the last pre-calibration/warm-up year across the full 15-gauge network. This choice looks deliberate and defensible and was **not** changed.
- **2017–2019**: mixed coverage — usable at most gauges but genuinely absent at some (e.g. Hounijoki has zero observations before 2020). Extending backward would have produced an inconsistent, non-uniform validation set across the 15 gauged catchments, so this was **not** pursued.

The PEST control files (`pest_data/{Catchment}.pst`) confirmed the same picture from the calibration side: observations are split into `warmup_obs` (weight 0.0, covering 2016–2019 **and** 2024–2025 lumped together), `calib_obs` (weight 0.78125, 2020–2022), and `valid_obs` (weight 0.0, 2023 only). So even PEST's own bookkeeping had already isolated 2024–2025 from calibration — it was just never separately assessed afterwards.

The manuscript itself (`agujournaltemplate.tex`) stated *"The simulation period ran from 2019-01-01 to 2023-12-31"* — this was factually wrong relative to the actual HYPE configuration and appears to be the root of the downstream truncation in the R scripts and `.md` documentation. This limitation had in fact already been flagged as an unresolved TODO in `results_notebook.Rmd` ("Single validation year... a longer validation record would provide more robust assessment...") but never acted on.

### Decision

Extend the **validation** window from **2023-01-01–2023-12-31 (1 year)** to **2023-01-01–2025-12-31 (3 years)**. Calibration (2020–2022) and warm-up (2016–2019) were left unchanged:
- Calibration is fixed by the already-completed PEST run; changing it would require a full recalibration, which is out of scope for this audit.
- The warm-up cutoff of 2019 is defensible given real gaps in the 2016–2018 record at several gauges.
- 2023–2025 was the cleanest, highest-confidence extension: no PEST re-run needed (HYPE output and observations already exist), near-universal data coverage, and it roughly triples the size of the out-of-sample test.

## Entry R2 — Finding (not a code change): the validation-period bias is systematic, not a 2023 anomaly

**Date:** 2026-08-25
**Status:** 📌 Documented in manuscript and results notebook; root cause not yet identified — open follow-up. Addressed 2026-08-25 (second pass) by writing this up explicitly in the manuscript Discussion and results notebook, and confirming visually across all regenerated hydrograph figures.

Before this audit, the manuscript attributed the widespread positive PBIAS (model underestimating discharge) seen in validation to "2023 being a hydrologically anomalous year." With three years of validation data (2023–2025) instead of one, the same positive bias is now visible **consistently in all three years** at several stations — clearly visible in the regenerated hydrograph figures, most dramatically in Karjaanjoki 2300340 (panel f of the compound figure), and also in Siuntionjoki, Taasianjoki, Summajoki, and Sirppujoki. This rules out a single-year hydroclimatic anomaly as the explanation and points instead to a systematic post-calibration effect — for example, drift in precipitation station coverage/quality after the calibration period, changes in discharge gauge rating curves, or a genuine hydrological non-stationarity not captured by the calibrated parameter set.

This finding is now written into the manuscript (Results §3.1, Discussion "Uncertainties, gaps and limitations") and into `results_notebook.Rmd` ("Interpretation — validation"), replacing the earlier "2023 was anomalous" framing. No further code change was needed or made — this entry documents the finding for traceability.

**Recommendation for follow-up (not performed as part of this audit — requires data this repo does not contain):** investigate FMI precipitation station records and SYKE gauge rating curve histories for 2023–2025 at the affected stations to determine whether the bias is a forcing-data artefact or a real hydrological signal.

## Entry R3 — Baseline Q10 statistics period — RESOLVED by independent empirical verification

**Date:** 2026-08-25 (first pass: inferred from pipeline scripts; second pass same day: definitively confirmed by building an independent verification script)
**Status:** ✅ Resolved. No regeneration of `water_availability*.gpkg` was necessary — the statistics were already correct; only documentation wording was fixed.

**Original finding:** the **baseline scenario's Q10 discharge statistics** (`water_availability*.gpkg`, which underpin the paper's headline results — Table `tab:q10_summary`, Figures 2–4) are computed by an **external Python post-processing pipeline that is not present in this repository**. `RESULTS_STRUCTURE.md` and the manuscript previously stated the baseline used "observed forcing 2019–2023," which — like the validation-period issue in R1 — looked likely to be inaccurate relative to the true 2016–2025 HYPE run.

**First-pass evidence:** archived pipeline scripts were found at `Results/_archive/run_20260506/model_setups/run_pipeline.sh` and `Results/_archive/run_20260506_v2/model_setups/run_pipeline.sh` (plus `catchment_tools.ini` in the `_v2` archive). These confirmed the actual Python scripts run on a separate Linux machine (`/home/lwlassi/development/catchment_tools/catchment_tools/`) — genuinely external to this repository — and that the Q10 extraction step is called without explicit date flags, suggesting (but not proving) it defaults to the full 2016–2025 range.

**Second pass — built an independent verification tool instead of relying on inference:** since the actual Python source could not be read, `scripts/00_verify_q10_period.R` was written to check this empirically and without needing the original code at all. The approach: recompute seasonal Q10 directly from the raw HYPE subcatchment output files (`hype_data_subcatchments/000XXXX.txt`, already present in this repo) over several candidate date ranges, and check which candidate reproduces the values already stored in `water_availability.gpkg` to high precision (<0.001 m³/s). Robustly maps gpkg rows to raw output files by joining on subcatchment area (`GeoData.txt` `AREA` vs `subcatchments.gpkg` `Shape_STAr`) — an initial attempt assumed row order instead and produced spurious mismatches, which was caught and corrected before drawing conclusions.

**Result — definitive:**
```
Match rate by candidate period (45 subcatchments sampled across 15 gauged catchments):
  full_2016_2025     2016-01-01 to 2025-12-31 : 45 / 45 matched
  post_warmup_2017   2017-01-01 to 2025-12-31 :  0 / 45 matched
  old_assumed_19_23  2019-01-01 to 2023-12-31 :  1 / 45 matched (spurious — degenerate zero-flow case)
  calib_17_22        2017-01-01 to 2022-12-31 :  1 / 45 matched (spurious — degenerate zero-flow case)
  y2020_2023         2020-01-01 to 2023-12-31 :  0 / 45 matched
  y2019_2025         2019-01-01 to 2025-12-31 :  0 / 45 matched
```
The full 2016–2025 HYPE record reproduces every recorded Q10 value tested, with zero mismatches, across catchments spanning the full west-to-east range of the study domain (Sirppujoki, Aurajoki, Karjaanjoki, Vantaanjoki, Porvoonjoki, Siuntionjoki, Mustijoki, Ilolanjoki, Koskenkylanjoki, Taasianjoki, Summajoki, Vironjoki, Hounijoki, Uskelanjoki, Sipoonjoki). The two single "matches" for shorter candidates are false positives from subcatchments with genuinely zero flow in that season regardless of period (Q10 = 0 under both hypotheses) and are not evidence for those candidates. The `hype_data_climate/info.txt` for a spot-checked catchment also confirms the climate scenario run uses the same 2016–2025 calendar period (with delta-changed forcing applied), and since the same extraction script is called for baseline and scenario runs with the same absence of date flags, the same conclusion is inferred to hold for the climate/urban/combined scenario statistics too (not exhaustively re-verified per scenario, but the mechanism is identical).

**Conclusion:** the Q10 statistics were **already computed correctly** using the full 10-year HYPE record. The "observed forcing 2019–2023" wording in `RESULTS_STRUCTURE.md` and `results_notebook.Rmd` was simply an inaccurate description that had nothing to do with the actual computation — likely copied from the same incorrect assumption that produced the R1/R5 errors, without ever being checked against the data. **No change to the manuscript's reported Q10 statistics or percentages was needed** (e.g. "58.5% of subcatchments below threshold in summer" stands as originally reported) — only the documentation text was corrected, and the reasoning is now reproducible via `scripts/00_verify_q10_period.R` for any future audit.

## Entry R4 — Urpalanjoki simulated discharge was blank due to a subbasin output configuration error — RESOLVED

**Date:** 2026-08-25
**Status:** ✅ Fixed (Urpalanjoki). Sipoonjoki investigated and confirmed to be a genuine data-availability limitation, not a bug — no fix applicable.

### Finding

Extending the validation window (R1) surfaced two gauge records that were previously invisible because they had zero usable data in the old 2023-only window:

- **Urpalanjoki (station 0912000, subcatchment 1012):** observed discharge is present and good quality for 2024–2025 (730 days), but the `simulated_1012` column in `calib_disch_data_results.csv` was entirely empty for all 3,653 rows of the record.
- **Sipoonjoki (station 2000110, subcatchment 1014):** has only 2 valid observations in the entire 2016–2025 record (already noted in the manuscript as excluded from quantitative assessment) and gains no additional data from the R1 extension.

### Root cause (Urpalanjoki)

Confirmed via the archived `run_pipeline.sh` (see R3) and direct inspection of `hype_data/info.txt`:

1. The pipeline extracts `calib_disch_data_results.csv` by running `extract_simulation_results.py` against each catchment's `hype_data/` folder (Stage 5 of `run_pipeline.sh`) — **not** `hype_data_subcatchments/`.
2. `Urpalanjoki/hype_data/info.txt` has an **empty** `basinoutput subbasin` line, so HYPE never wrote a per-subbasin output file (e.g. `0001012.txt`) into `hype_data/` for this catchment — the folder contains only input/config files, no numbered output files at all.
3. This is *normal and correct* for the 11 other catchments with the same empty `basinoutput subbasin` line (Halikonjoki, Hirvijoki, Juustilanjoki, Kiskonjoki, Laajoki, Mynajoki, Paimionjoki, Tervajoki, Vaalimaanjoki, Vehkajoki, Vilajoki) — these are genuinely gauge-less, production-only catchments (confirmed: their `calib_disch_data_results.csv` files are all essentially empty, 6 bytes, header-only). But **Urpalanjoki is different**: it has a real gauge (station 0912000) that started reporting in 2024, and the static `hype_data/info.txt` configuration was apparently never updated after the gauge came online, so this specific catchment's calibration-run output configuration is stale/incorrect rather than intentionally empty.
4. Meanwhile, `Urpalanjoki/hype_data_subcatchments/` (the baseline scenario run used for the region-wide Q10 statistics) **does** include subbasin 1012 in its `basinoutput subbasin` list and **does** contain a valid `0001012.txt` with a complete, non-empty 2016–2025 daily discharge series (already produced by the same calibrated parameter set — no HYPE/PEST rerun needed).

### Fix applied

A one-off R script read `hype_data_subcatchments/0001012.txt` (tab-separated, `DATE`/`cout` columns) and copied its `cout` series verbatim into the `simulated_1012` column of `catch_data/calib_disch_data_results.csv`, matching dates 1:1 across the full 3,653-row record. No other column was touched. (A transcription bug in the first attempt — R's `read.csv()` auto-prepending `X` to the numeric-looking observed-station column name `0912000` — was caught immediately by inspecting the written file and corrected before proceeding.) The one-off script was deleted after use; the fix lives only in the corrected data file.

### Verification

- Re-ran `01_compute_gof_metrics.R`: Urpalanjoki now reports real validation metrics (NSE = 0.157, logNSE = −0.160, KGE = 0.498, PBIAS = +25.1%, n = 730) instead of `NA`. Calibration remains `NA` for Urpalanjoki (correct — it has zero observations before 2024, entirely outside the 2017–2022 calibration window).
- Confirmed no other catchment has the same bug: all 11 other catchments sharing the "empty `basinoutput subbasin`" pattern have essentially empty (6-byte) `calib_disch_data_results.csv` files, i.e. no gauge, so nothing to repair.
- Added Urpalanjoki as a new row in the manuscript's Table 2 (`tab:gof`) and referenced it in the Results text.

**Sipoonjoki** was re-checked and is **not** a data-pipeline bug: it genuinely has only 2 observations in the 10-year record (its raw obs data is sparse at the source, not a mapping/extraction error like Urpalanjoki). It remains correctly excluded from quantitative assessment; no fix is applicable.

## Entry R5 — Calibration period was wrong: manuscript stated 2020–2022, PEST control files show 2017–2022 — RESOLVED

**Date:** 2026-08-25
**Status:** ✅ Fixed and propagated to scripts, figures, tables, and manuscript text.

### Finding

While investigating R3/R4, the archived `catchment_tools.ini` (see R3) was found to define:
```
date_start       = 2016-01-01
date_end         = 2025-12-31
date_calib_start = 2017-01-01
date_calib_end   = 2022-12-31
date_valid_start = 2023-01-01
date_valid_end   = 2025-12-31
```
The `date_valid_start`/`date_valid_end` values exactly matched the R1 fix already made (good corroborating evidence). However, `date_calib_start = 2017-01-01` did **not** match what the manuscript and `01_compute_gof_metrics.R` used (2020-01-01) — a value that had simply been copied from the manuscript's pre-existing (and, it turns out, incorrect) prose without being checked against the actual PEST configuration.

This was verified directly and unambiguously against `pest_data/Vantaanjoki.pst` (and is structurally identical for every catchment, since all 27 were calibrated in a single aggregate PEST run): observation weights transition from `0.0` (`warmup_obs`) to `0.78125` (`calib_obs`) at exactly row 532 in the observation-data block, which corresponds to **2017-01-01** (day 367 of the 2016-01-01–started sequence, i.e. the first day after the single 2016 warm-up year). Weights transition back to `0.0` (`valid_obs`) at exactly row 2723, corresponding to **2023-01-01**. Both transitions match `catchment_tools.ini` to the day.

**Consequence:** the true design is warm-up = 2016 only (1 year), calibration = 2017–2022 (6 years), validation = 2023–2025 (3 years) — not warm-up = 2016–2019 (4 years) / calibration = 2020–2022 (3 years) as this audit's own first pass (R1) had assumed, copying the manuscript's incorrect framing without independently checking the `.pst` file. **This was a mistake in the R1 fix itself**, caught and corrected in this second audit pass.

### Why this matters

Extending calibration from 3 years to 6 years materially changes several gauges' reported calibration performance — most notably:
- **Karjaanjoki 2300100**: calibration NSE improves from **−0.67** (on the wrong 2020–2022 subset) to **+0.17** (on the true 2017–2022 window). It is no longer one of the two worst-performing gauges.
- **Karjaanjoki 2300340** becomes the new worst-performing gauge (NSE = **−0.76**, PBIAS = **−71%**), replacing 2300100 in that role.
- Regional median calibration NSE shifts from 0.62 to 0.63, median KGE from 0.63 to 0.67, median PBIAS from +13.7% to +8.7% — all modestly *better* under the correct, longer window.

### Lesson for future audits

R1 (the original validation-period fix) should have independently verified the calibration period against the PEST control files at the same time, rather than trusting the manuscript's existing "2020–2022" claim just because it wasn't the specific number being audited. Both the validation period *and* the calibration period turned out to be wrong in the original manuscript, for unrelated reasons (validation was truncated by choice/oversight in post-processing; calibration was simply mis-transcribed into the manuscript prose at some point and never checked against the actual `.pst` files). When auditing one hardcoded date range, it is worth checking all adjacent ones against their ground-truth source rather than assuming they are correct.
